# Reading COT data for trades

COT data shows how crowded a trade is. It doesn't time entries. Positions are as of Tuesday and published Friday, so they're three days old on arrival, and extreme positioning can persist for months. Use it to decide which trades to look for, and use price to decide when to act.

This explains how COT data is commonly used. It is not financial advice or a recommendation to take any trade.

## Common approaches

1. **Fade extremes, only once price confirms.** A speculator COT index above 90 or below 10 means a crowded trade. The edge comes when price starts turning against the crowd, for example a higher low while specs are at a record short.
2. **Watch commercials in commodities.** In metals, energy and grains, commercials (producers and users hedging) tend to be heavily net long near bottoms and heavily net short near tops. This works much less well for currencies and stock indices.
3. **Confirm trends.** In a healthy uptrend specs keep adding longs. Price making new highs while the speculator net long shrinks is a warning sign.
4. **Large weekly changes and open interest.** Big one-week shifts show conviction. Rising price with rising open interest means new money is coming in; rising price with falling open interest means shorts are covering.

## Weekly routine

Friday after 21:30 SA time, or Saturday:
1. Check the tracker has the new week.
2. Scan the market board for COT index readings above 90 or below 10, and unusually large weekly changes.
3. Compare each one against price on a weekly chart.
4. Only consider a trade where positioning and price structure agree. Set stops from price levels, not from COT.

## Pitfalls

- **Currency futures are quoted against the US dollar.** A record euro short is effectively a crowded long-dollar position, so read EUR, GBP and AUD together.
- **The rand is a small market on CME** and says little about the much bigger spot and offshore rand market.
- **Legacy report only.** "Non-commercial" combines hedge funds with other large traders. The CFTC's Disaggregated (commodities: managed money) and Traders in Financial Futures (currencies and indices: leveraged funds) reports split them out.
- **52-week COT index.** Many traders also check a 3-year (156-week) window, because one year can make an ordinary position look extreme.
- **Test before trusting it.** With 93 weeks stored, check how past readings above 90 or below 10 actually played out in your markets.
